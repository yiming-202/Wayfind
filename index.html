Before you leave

Use your clinic's own appointment message as the source of truth. This checklist helps you organize, but it does not replace the clinic's instructions.

  • Check the appointment date, time, and facility address.
  • Gather the confirmation and any documents the clinic specifically asked you to bring.
  • Write down questions you want to ask.
  • If you need an accommodation, contact the facility to ask how to arrange it.
Helpful next action: Open your appointment confirmation and check the location and arrival instructions.

When you arrive

First, confirm that you are at the correct building and entrance. The sample directions in Wayfind are not live facility data.

  • Look for the facility's information desk or check-in signs.
  • Ask staff to confirm the accessible entrance, elevator, or waiting area if needed.
  • If the route is blocked or does not work for you, pause and ask staff for an alternative.
“Hi, I have an appointment. Could you help me find the accessible way to the check-in desk?”

At check-in

Show your appointment confirmation or share the details the receptionist asks for. You do not need to share more personal information than the facility requires.

“I’m here for my appointment. I also have an access need I’d like to discuss. Could you tell me who can help arrange that?”
  • Ask where you should wait.
  • Tell staff if you need communication support, extra time, or help navigating.
  • Ask who to speak with if the support you need is not available at the desk.

During your visit

You can ask the care team to explain information in a way that works for you. If something is unclear, it is okay to ask them to repeat it.

“Could you explain that in simpler steps, or write down the important instructions so I can review them?”
  • Ask what the next step is and who is responsible for it.
  • Ask staff to clarify unfamiliar words or instructions.
  • Before leaving the room, check that you understand where you need to go next.

Before you leave

Use the instructions given by your care team. Wayfind cannot interpret personal medical instructions or decide what care you need.

  • Make sure you know the next step your care team asked you to take.
  • Ask who to contact if you have questions about the instructions.
  • Check whether a follow-up appointment or further information is needed.
  • Ask for written or accessible copies of information if that would help.
“Before I go, could you review my next steps and tell me who I should contact if I have questions?”

If plans change

What if I get stuck?

A helpful tool should never leave someone stranded when a route, entrance, or instruction does not match reality.

Pause and get a reliable answer.If a lift is out of service, a corridor is blocked, signs are confusing, or the route does not feel accessible, stop and ask the information desk or clinic staff to confirm an alternative. Do not follow an unverified route just because the app suggests it.

A sentence you can use

“I’m trying to get to my appointment, but this route isn't working for my access needs. Could you show me an accessible way to get there or help me contact the clinic?”

The 20-second pitch

Healthcare navigation should work for everyone.

People with disabilities can face extra uncertainty when navigating a large medical facility. Wayfind is an accessibility-first hospital navigation concept that turns a destination and a person's preferences into clear, step-by-step directions.

What makes it different: it starts with the patient's access needs instead of assuming everyone can use the same route.

Wayfind is an educational prototype, not a validated navigation service. Route examples are fictional. A real deployment would require facility-approved accessibility data and testing with disabled users.
